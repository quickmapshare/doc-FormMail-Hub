Standard Google Workspace accounts enforce daily email limits that restrict customer communication. By configuring a **Custom SMTP (Simple Mail Transfer Protocol)** server in FormMail Hub, you can scale automated email dispatch from 500 to over 10,000+ emails per day while preserving personal sending quotas and maintaining maximum deliverability.

This guide provides step-by-step configuration instructions for popular transactional email providers and detailed domain authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) setup instructions.

---

## 1. Provider Configuration Guides

### Provider A: Amazon SES (Simple Email Service)
Amazon SES offers high deliverability and low costs for high-volume email triggers.

1. **Verify Your Domain in AWS:**
   - Log into the AWS Management Console and navigate to **Amazon SES**.
   - Under **Configuration** > **Verified Identities**, click **Create Identity**.
   - Select **Domain**, enter your domain name (e.g., `yourcompany.com`), and choose your DKIM key settings.
   - Publish the generated CNAME records in your domain's DNS manager (Cloudflare, Route53, GoDaddy, etc.).
2. **Generate SMTP Credentials:**
   - In the Amazon SES console, navigate to **Account Level Settings** > **SMTP Settings**.
   - Click **Create SMTP Credentials**.
   - AWS will generate an IAM user with dedicated **SMTP Username** and **SMTP Password** credentials. Download or copy these credentials immediately.
3. **Request Production Access (Exit Sandbox):**
   - By default, new AWS SES accounts are in the Sandbox environment (limited to verified recipient emails).
   - Request production access under **Account Details** > **Request Production Access** to send emails to any recipient.
4. **Configure FormMail Hub:**
   - In FormMail Hub, select **Settings** > **SMTP Configuration** > **Amazon SES**.
   - **Host:** `email-smtp.us-east-1.amazonaws.com` (adjust region as appropriate).
   - **Port:** `587` (TLS) or `465` (SSL).
   - **Username:** Your AWS SES SMTP Username.
   - **Password:** Your AWS SES SMTP Password.

---

### Provider B: Google Workspace & Gmail™ App Passwords
To use a custom Gmail™ or Google Workspace account without hitting OAuth scope limitations, use an **App Password**.

1. **Enable 2-Step Verification:**
   - Go to your Google Account security settings (`myaccount.google.com/security`).
   - Under "How you sign in to Google", ensure **2-Step Verification** is turned **ON**.
2. **Create an App Password:**
   - Search for **App Passwords** in the Google Account search bar.
   - Enter an app name (e.g., `FormMail Hub SMTP`).
   - Click **Create** and copy the generated 16-character secret key.
3. **Configure FormMail Hub:**
   - In FormMail Hub, select **Settings** > **SMTP Configuration** > **Gmail / Google Workspace**.
   - **Host:** `smtp.gmail.com`
   - **Port:** `587` (TLS) or `465` (SSL)
   - **Username:** Your full Gmail or Workspace email address (e.g., `sales@yourcompany.com`).
   - **Password:** The generated 16-character App Password (spaces excluded).

---

### Provider C: SendGrid
1. Log into your **SendGrid** account and navigate to **Settings** > **API Keys**.
2. Click **Create API Key**, assign **Full Access** or **Restricted Access** (with Mail Send permissions), and copy the key.
3. Navigate to **Settings** > **Sender Authentication** to verify your domain via CNAME DNS records.
4. **FormMail Hub Settings:**
   - **Host:** `smtp.sendgrid.net`
   - **Port:** `587`
   - **Username:** `apikey` (literal string)
   - **Password:** Your generated SendGrid API Key

## 2. Domain Authentication (SPF, DKIM & DMARC)

To ensure emails sent via custom SMTP land directly in the recipient's **Primary Inbox** and avoid spam filters, configure these essential DNS records:

### SPF (Sender Policy Framework)
SPF specifies which server IP addresses are authorized to send email on behalf of your domain.
- Add a DNS `TXT` record at your root domain (`@`):
  ```txt
  v=spf1 include:amazonses.com include:_spf.google.com ~all
  ```
*(Include only the providers active on your domain).* 

### DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail)
DKIM attaches a cryptographic signature to every outbound email.
- Copy the CNAME or TXT keys generated in your SMTP provider's dashboard (e.g., Amazon SES or SendGrid) and insert them into your DNS provider.

### DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication)
DMARC tells receiving inbox providers how to handle emails that fail SPF or DKIM checks.
- Add a DNS `TXT` record at `_dmarc.yourdomain.com`:
  ```txt
  v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@yourdomain.com
  ```

## 3. Testing & Troubleshooting

| Error / Symptom | Possible Cause | Resolution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `535 5.7.8 Authentication Failed` | Incorrect credentials or lack of App Password. | Re-check credentials. Ensure Google Workspace users use an **App Password**, not standard password. |
| `Connection Timeout on Port 25` | Port 25 blocked by cloud ISP or firewall. | Switch connection port to **587** (STARTTLS) or **465** (SSL). |
| `Email Delivered to Spam` | Unverified domain or missing SPF/DKIM DNS records. | Complete Sender Domain Verification and add valid SPF and DKIM records. |
| `SES Messaging Account in Sandbox` | Amazon SES sandbox restriction. | Submit a SES Production Access request in the AWS Console. |
